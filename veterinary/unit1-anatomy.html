General Osteology

Bones form the body's framework.

Five bone types by shape

Long - Short - Flat - Irregular - Sesamoid

Little Sisters Fight In Snow

Examples: Long=femur/humerus | Short=carpals/tarsals | Flat=scapula/skull | Irregular=vertebrae | Sesamoid=patella

Bone properties: Hard yet slightly elastic; collagen gives tensile strength, hydroxyapatite gives compression strength. Remodeled by osteoblasts (build) and osteoclasts (break down).
Bone structure: Periosteum (outer) -> compact bone -> spongy bone -> medullary cavity (yellow marrow in adults).
Bone markings: Processes (projections), depressions (cavities), openings (foramina, fissures, meatuses, sinuses).

General Arthrology (Joints)

Joints are where bones meet and move.

Three main joint classifications

Fibrous - Cartilaginous - Synovial

Frozen Couch Slides

Fibrous=immovable (skull sutures) | Cartilaginous=slightly movable (vertebrae) | Synovial=freely movable (diarthrodial)

Diarthrodial (synovial) joint structure: Articular capsule + synovial membrane (secretes synovial fluid) + articular cartilage + joint cavity. Reinforced by ligaments.

Six synovial joint types

Hinge, Pivot, Ball, Saddle, Gliding, Condyloid

Common movements: Flexion (decrease angle) • Extension (increase angle) • Abduction (away from midline) • Adduction (toward midline) • Rotation (around axis) • Circumduction (conical circle).

General Myology

Muscles are the body's engines of movement.

Three muscle tissue types

Skeletal - Cardiac - Smooth

Some Cats Sleep

TypeStriationsControlLocation
SkeletalStriatedVoluntaryAttached to bones
CardiacStriatedInvoluntaryHeart only
SmoothNon-striatedInvoluntaryHollow organs, vessels
Muscle etymology: Named by location, shape, size, action, origin/insertion, or fiber direction. E.g., biceps = two heads, longissimus = longest, rectus = straight.
Tendons: connect muscle to bone. Ligaments: connect bone to bone. Aponeuroses: flat sheet-like tendons. Synovial bursae: fluid sacs reducing friction. Tendon sheaths: tubular bursae around tendons.
Muscle attachments: Origin = fixed/proximal end • Insertion = movable/distal end.

Angiology - Heart and Circulation

The heart pumps life. Trace the loop.

Heart chambers and valve flow

Right Atrium -> Right Ventricle -> Lungs -> Left Atrium -> Left Ventricle -> Body

Valves: Tricuspid (RA-RV) • Pulmonary (RV-pulmonary artery) • Mitral/Bicuspid (LA-LV) • Aortic (LV-aorta)

Try Pulling My Aorta

Pulmonary circulation: Right ventricle -> pulmonary artery -> lungs (oxygenation) -> pulmonary veins -> left atrium. Deoxygenated blood only.
Systemic circulation: Left ventricle -> aorta -> body tissues -> vena cavae -> right atrium. Oxygenated blood to body.

Lymphatic vs venous system

Veins: carry blood to heart (mostly deoxygenated except pulmonary). Have valves.

Lymphatics: carry lymph (interstitial fluid) to venous system. No RBCs. One-way toward heart.

Major arteries: Aorta (systemic), pulmonary artery (lungs), facial, femoral, coccygeal, cephalic, saphenous.

Major veins: Jugular (neck), cephalic (foreleg), lateral saphenous (hindleg), coccygeal (tail).

Neurology - Nervous System

The body's command center. Map the wires.

Nervous system architecture

CNS = Brain + Spinal Cord

PNS = Cranial nerves + Spinal nerves + Ganglia

ANS = Sympathetic + Parasympathetic (involuntary)

CNS is Central, PNS is Peripheral, ANS is Automatic

Meninges (outside to inside)

Dura, Arachnoid, Pia

Dura mater (tough outer) -> Arachnoid mater (web-like middle) -> Pia mater (delicate inner, hugs brain)

CSF circulates in the subarachnoid space.

Spinal nerve formation: Dorsal root (sensory, has dorsal root ganglion) + Ventral root (motor) -> mixed spinal nerve -> rami -> plexuses -> peripheral nerves.
Brain: Cerebrum (thought, voluntary) • Cerebellum (coordination, balance) • Brainstem (pons, medulla, midbrain - vital autonomic functions).
Sense organs (Aesthesiology): Eye (vision), Ear (hearing + balance), Nose (olfaction), Tongue (taste), Skin (touch). Receptors detect stimuli and send signals via afferent nerves to CNS.

Splanchnology - Body Cavities and Organs

Splanchnology is the study of the viscera (internal organs).

Three main body cavities

Thoracic -> Abdominal -> Pelvic

Thoracic: heart, lungs, thymus, esophagus, trachea

Abdominal: stomach, liver, spleen, intestines, kidneys, pancreas

Pelvic: urinary bladder, reproductive organs, rectum

Diagnostic Imaging

See inside without cutting open.
Radiography (X-ray): best for BONE. Dense structures appear white (radiopaque). Air appears black (radiolucent). Quick, cheap, great for fractures.
Ultrasound: best for SOFT TISSUE and fluid. Real-time, no radiation. Great for pregnancy, heart (echocardiogram), abdominal organs.

X-ray vs ultrasound - quick pick

Suspect fracture? X-ray

Check pregnancy / soft organ? Ultrasound

Lungs / chest? X-ray

Heart function / fluid? Ultrasound

Surface Landmarks - Palpation and Clinical Sites

Where to touch, poke, and needle - safely.
Palpable bony prominences: tuber coxae (hip point), tuber ischii (seat bone), olecranon (elbow point), patella, spinous processes of vertebrae, caudal vertebrae (tail head).
Lymph nodes: mandibular, prescapular, axillary, inguinal, popliteal - commonly enlarged in disease.
Arteries for palpation: facial (jaw), femoral (groin), coccygeal (tail).
Venipuncture sites: jugular vein (neck), cephalic vein (foreleg), lateral saphenous vein (hindleg), coccygeal vein (tail). Jugular is most common for large volumes.
Bone marrow aspiration: sternum, iliac crest (wing of ilium), greater tubercle of humerus.

CSF collection: atlanto-occipital space (back of skull), lumbosacral space (lower back).
